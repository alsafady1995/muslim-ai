# 1. Psychology first, then hooks

Screenshots, copy and ads are downstream of one question: *what does this person feel before they open the store,
and what do they want to feel after?* This file answers that, challenges the brief where it is wrong, then picks the
hook that every other asset is built on.

---

## 1.2 The nine questions

### 1. What emotional problem are users trying to solve?

**Doubt without a witness.** Recitation and memorization are judged by ear, and you cannot reliably hear your own
mistakes. So a believer who has not recited to anyone for years carries a low, constant uncertainty about the words
they say in every prayer and about the surahs they once memorized. It is not dramatic. It is a pebble in the shoe that
never goes away, and it surfaces at specific moments: leading a prayer, a quiet night in Ramadan, hearing a child recite.

The desired state is **طمأنينة**: calm certainty. "I checked. I know where I stand."

### 2. What motivates them to improve?

- **Worship quality.** The Quran recited in salah; wanting it to be right is a devotional motive, not a vanity one.
- **Reward and closeness.** The well-known hadith about the one who is skilled with the Quran and the one who struggles
  with it and still gets a double reward. Struggle is honoured, which matters for tone: we celebrate effort, never shame.
- **Identity and legacy.** "حافظ" is an honoured identity in the Gulf. Parents want it for their children, and adults
  want to recover the part of it they lost.
- **Seasonal resolve.** Ramadan, the last ten nights, Dhul Hijjah, the new Hijri year. Motivation spikes; the habit fails
  in week two.

### 3. What do they fear?

| Fear | How it shows up | What we must never do with it |
|---|---|---|
| **Having been wrong for years** (in salah, unknowingly) | The single strongest curiosity driver | Never weaponize it ("Is your prayer even valid?"). Religious-guilt marketing is unethical and gets reported |
| **Being heard getting it wrong** | Adults avoid tasmee' with people out of embarrassment | Promise privacy: only the coach listens, recordings are deleted |
| **Losing what they memorized** | "I had Al-Mulk, now I stumble after the fifth ayah" | Use it honestly: show their *own* at-risk ayahs, never a generic scare |
| **Starting again and failing again** | Ramadan restarts that die in week two | Small daily plan, streak freezes, no punishment for a missed day |
| **The AI being wrong about the Quran** | Distrust of AI on sacred text | Show sources, never claim accuracy we have not measured, let the user mark a wrong flag |
| **Being sold to** | "Every Islamic app wants my money or shows me ads" | No paywall in onboarding, no ads, honest trial reminder |

### 4. What do existing apps fail to solve?

- **They are libraries, not listeners.** Muslim Pro and Quran Majeed give you text, audio and prayer times. Nothing
  listens to you.
- **The one that listens is priced like a tutor.** Tarteel charges SAR 349.99 a year in Saudi Arabia.
- **Nobody manages forgetting.** Apps help you memorize *new* ayahs. Almost nobody tells you which old ayahs you are
  losing this week. Review, not memorization, is where hifz dies.
- **Ads and noise around sacred text.** The two largest apps run ads with third-party tracking next to the Quran.
- **English-first design** translated into Arabic, for a market where the users think, search and pray in Arabic.

### 5. Why choose Asmi3ni?

Because it does the one thing a teacher does that apps don't: **it listens, and then it remembers for you.** Recite,
see the slips word by word, and the next review is scheduled before the ayah fades. Arabic-first, no ads, about a third
of Tarteel's price.

### 6. What creates immediate curiosity?

A **personal, testable unknown with a near-zero cost to find out**: *"What would a careful listener hear in my
Al-Fatiha?"* Everyone knows Al-Fatiha, it takes under a minute, the onboarding check is free and not counted against any
limit. The gap between "I'm sure it's fine" and "let me just check" is the install.

### 7. What creates an emotional response?

- **Recognition**: the word "أسمِعني" itself. It is what a teacher says. It triggers a childhood memory in seconds.
- **The 11 pm moment**: wanting to review and having no one to recite to.
- **Seeing your own words coloured on screen**: green, then one amber word. It is intimate, and it is *yours*.
- **"Alhamdulillah, Surah Al-Mulk is safe"**: relief, not achievement.

### 8. What creates trust?

1. **Specifics over adjectives**: "missed, wrong, extra and unclear words", "recordings deleted after analysis",
   "answers cite their ayah". Never "advanced AI".
2. **Humility about the machine**: "a practice partner between lessons with your teacher", not "your sheikh".
3. **Visible honesty in money**: free plan that is useful, trial reminder before any charge, "Continue free" as large as
   the buy button.
4. **No ads, no ad tracking** next to the Quran.
5. **Real social proof only.** At launch we have none. Do not fake it. Use the founder's story and real beta quotes
   (with permission) until ratings exist.
6. **Arabic written for Arabs**: copy that reads like a Gulf teacher speaking, not like a translation.

### 9. What creates the desire to install now?

**A low-effort, high-certainty first win, today.** "Recite Al-Fatiha. In under a minute you'll see your recitation
word by word." The install is not a commitment to memorize the Quran; it is a 60-second check. Commitment comes later,
from the plan.

## 1.3 Assumptions in the brief we reject

| The brief says | Why it is wrong or risky | What we do instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Users get real-time feedback" | Not true today. The coach analyses after you stop (seconds later). Tarteel *is* live, and a reviewer comparing the two would call us out | Say "word by word" and "in seconds", never "real-time" or "live". Market live mode the day it ships |
| "Improve Tajweed" | Tajweed is a "possible short madd" hint only. Claiming tajweed coaching to a Gulf audience, many of whom studied tajweed, is the fastest route to 1-star reviews | Drop tajweed from all headlines and keywords until Phase 2 ships |
| "Users feel they have a personal Quran teacher" | The *feeling* is right, the *claim* is wrong. "AI teacher of the Quran" offends some scholars and invites App Review scrutiny | "The listener between lessons." The app sits beside the teacher, so teachers can recommend it |
| "Detect mistakes" as the headline | It is the right curiosity hook, but the accuracy is not measured. One false flag on Al-Fatiha in onboarding loses the user and the review | Lead with the act (tasmee'), not the verdict. Run the accuracy benchmark before paid acquisition (see `08-launch-campaign.md` §2) |
| "Competitor destruction" | Naming competitors in store metadata breaks App Store rule 2.3.7 and Google Play metadata policy; attacking other Muslim apps reads badly in this community | Position by contrast in our own channels, with facts, never insults. Store copy never names a competitor |
| "Every headline maximizes installs and subscriptions" | Install hooks and subscription hooks are different jobs | Install = **be heard** (curiosity, low effort). Subscribe = **keep it** (loss aversion with the user's own data) |
| "Track progress" as a selling point | Everyone claims it; it converts no one | Show one concrete number: "6 ayahs at risk this week" |
| The name "ASMI3NI" | The "3" (Arabizi) is natural to young Gulf users but unreadable to others, and nobody will type it correctly in search | Always pair it with "أسمِعني" in Arabic stores. Add spelling variants to the keyword field (§3 of `03-store-listings.md`) |

## 1.5 The winner: hook #1

> **سمّع حفظك متى شئت… واعرف أين تعثّرت، كلمة بكلمة.**
> **Recite from memory any time. See exactly where you slipped, word by word.**

### Why it beats the others

1. **It names a ritual, not a feature.** "سمّع" is a verb every Gulf Muslim learned as a child. Zero explanation cost:
   the reader already knows what will happen. "AI recitation analysis" needs explaining; "tasmee'" doesn't.
2. **It removes the scarce resource.** The bottleneck in hifz was never the Quran or the will. It was *a listener with
   time*. "متى شئت / any time" removes it in two words.
3. **It opens a curiosity gap without fear.** "أين تعثّرت / where you slipped" assumes a slip (true for almost
   everyone) but frames it as a stumble, not a sin. It invites a check; it does not accuse. Hook #6 has more raw
   curiosity but leans on fear, and hook #2 stops the scroll but does not say what the app does.
4. **It is believable because it is concrete.** "Word by word" describes the *format* of the result, which is
   verifiable on screenshot 1, rather than claiming an accuracy level we have not measured.
5. **It is true today.** No "live", no "tajweed", no "teacher". It survives App Review, scholars, and a skeptical
   Tarteel user.
6. **It serves all three paying segments.** A ("I used to know it"), B ("between halaqa sessions") and C ("check
   privately") all read themselves into it.
7. **It matches the first session exactly.** The ad promises tasmee' word by word; the store shows it; onboarding asks
   for Al-Fatiha and shows it. **Message match** from ad to first value is the single biggest lever on install-to-
   activation, and this hook is the only one where the promise *is* the first screen.
8. **It is the brand.** The app is called "let me hear you". The hook and the name say the same thing, so every
   impression builds the brand and the brand sells the hook.

### What would make this hook fail (and the fix)

| Risk | Fix |
|---|---|
| The first result is a false flag on Al-Fatiha | Ship the accuracy benchmark and an "unclear" state for low-confidence words before paid acquisition; add "this word was right" |
| The user recites perfectly and sees no mistake, so "nothing happened" | The result must celebrate ("كل كلمة سليمة. ما شاء الله") and immediately offer the next step: "Now try a surah you memorized long ago" |
| "Any time" with 10 free checks a week feels like a bait | Free plan copy states the limit plainly on the store page; the onboarding check is free and not counted |
